My sister told me about a blues artist called Dusty Strings. I played it, and I liked it. Strong, loud, a lot of power in every song.

So I went to YouTube to see Dusty Strings play. I found no live videos. Only album covers. That is when I started to smell a rat.

## SOHO Blues Band and Dusty Strings: two AI blues bands I like

Dusty Strings is an AI-generated artist, and so is SOHO Blues Band, the band YouTube suggested to me a few weeks later. My sister didn't know either. On Spotify, many Dusty Strings songs are credited together with DJ N3M3ZIZ and N3Bass Records.

SOHO Blues Band sounds a lot like Dusty Strings, but less pumped. Calmer. I liked it so much that I put "Midnight" in the Listening box on this site on 2 November. Then I checked, and it was AI too.

I still listen to both. The music is lovely, and I'm not going to pretend otherwise. But I want to know when it's AI, and it turns out that takes about five minutes. Spotify's label won't always do it for you.

## Spotify's AI Persona label: one of them has it

Spotify labels Dusty Strings as an AI Persona, but not SOHO Blues Band. These are the numbers from the two artist pages, [SOHO Blues Band](https://open.spotify.com/artist/3pRAhuz8fKrLHLBOwRUf80) and [Dusty Strings](https://open.spotify.com/artist/4NX07TyRa1HVUtQ8VjxbOJ), on 16 November 2026:

| | SOHO Blues Band | Dusty Strings |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly listeners | 676,200 | 70,700 |
| AI Persona label | No | Yes |
| AI in the Spotify credits | Yes | Not checked |

So the band with almost ten times as many listeners has no label, even though its own credits name AI as the singer and the composer.

Spotify [announced the label on 11 August 2026](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2026-08-11/ai-persona-badges-transparency/). It says: "This badge is about the artist's public identity, not about how the music was made." Spotify's team looks for an identity that "appears to represent photorealistic AI-generated identities". Labelled artists are left out of editorial and algorithmic recommendations by default, unless you follow them.

Audience size doesn't explain the gap: Spotify starts with artists above an audience threshold, and SOHO Blues Band has the bigger audience. The pictures might. Dusty Strings has a photorealistic woman on the profile. SOHO Blues Band has a stylised trumpet player that looks illustrated to me. That is my reading of Spotify's rule, not something Spotify told me.

The label answers "is this person real?". It does not answer "is this music made by AI?". For that, you read the credits.

## Track credits: where the answer is

The track credits tell you whether a song is AI: on SOHO Blues Band's songs they name AI as the singer and the composer. On Spotify, play a song and look at the Credits box in the panel on the right, or open the three dots on a track and pick "View credits".

YouTube shows the same thing in the video description. Songs that a distributor sends to YouTube get an automatic description, and the one for ["Want It All"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2dXiJ1lJDzs) says "Provided to YouTube by DistroKid" and then lists the credits:

"On Fire" from the same album has the exact same credits. One person, Andrew Curran, is listed as studio producer. I found nothing else about him, and the [Juno-winning producer Andy Curran](https://www.audeze.com/blogs/audeze-artists/audeze-interviews-musician-composer-and-producer-andy-curran) is someone else.

## Check an artist in five minutes: a checklist

Five checks tell you in about five minutes whether an artist is AI. I ran them on SOHO Blues Band and Dusty Strings. You don't need all of them. Two or three that point the same way are enough.

1. **Read the credits.** On Spotify, the Credits box or "View credits". On YouTube, the description under a song that says "Provided to YouTube by". Look for names like "GenerativeAI" or "AI" in the singer or composer role.
2. **Look for the label.** The AI Persona label sits under the artist name on Spotify. YouTube Music shows a "Gen AI" label on the player page. If one is there, you're done. If it's missing, keep going.
3. **Count the releases.** SOHO Blues Band released two albums with 17 songs each, ten weeks apart, in July and September 2026, and a third is on countdown. Dusty Strings has 6 albums and 318 singles on Spotify since February 2025. That is about 15 releases a month.
4. **Look for people.** Search for concerts, interviews, live videos, and band members. I found no live videos of either act, and no concerts, interviews, or members for SOHO Blues Band. The label on SOHO's albums, Bailar, didn't show up anywhere either.
5. **Look at the pictures and videos.** An artist with only album covers and no video of anyone playing is worth checking. A music video can give it away too: the official video for Dusty Strings' ["High Noon Protocol"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ThLX0aqcsiw) makes it plain that it is AI.

If you'd rather have Claude do the legwork, paste this into a Claude chat with web search on:

```text
Check whether the music artist [artist name] on [Spotify or YouTube] is AI-generated.
Look for: AI names in the track credits (Spotify credits or the "Provided to YouTube by" description), Spotify's AI Persona label, how many releases they have and how fast they came, and any trace of real people (concerts, interviews, live videos, named band members).
Tell me what you found for each point with a link, and then give me your verdict: AI, human, or unclear. Don't guess; say unclear when the evidence is thin.
```

I tested it on SOHO Blues Band with Claude Sonnet 5.5 and web search on 16 November. It said AI, with links. It couldn't open Spotify's pages, so it found the credits on Shazam and Apple Music instead, and it said plainly what it couldn't check. It also found "Songer AI" credited on songs by other, unrelated artists. That is a tool, not a singer.

## FAQ

### Is it wrong to like AI-generated music?

No. I like both of these acts, and I still listen to them. I also make the covers on this blog with a local AI image model, and I wrote about [how a nightly job picks that model](/blog/2026-11-14-local-image-model-scout/). What I want is to know what I'm listening to, so I can decide for myself.

### Why doesn't Spotify label SOHO Blues Band?

I don't know. Spotify says the AI Persona label is about the artist's identity and that its team looks for photorealistic AI identities. SOHO Blues Band's pictures look illustrated to me, and that may be why. That is my reading of the rule; Spotify hasn't said anything about this artist.

### Where do I find the credits for a song on Spotify?

Play the song and look at the Credits box in the panel on the right. On a track row, the three dots menu has "View credits".

### Does YouTube label AI music?

YouTube Music [started labelling songs](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/14/youtube-music-launches-new-labels-as-ai-generated-music-floods-streaming-platforms/) "Fully Gen AI", "Partly Gen AI" or "No Gen AI" on 14 October 2026, based on what the music partners declare and on YouTube's own detection. "Want It All" had no such label on its YouTube Music player page on 16 November. The automatic description with the credits is still where SOHO Blues Band's AI vocal and composer are listed.
